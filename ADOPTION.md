# Adoption, amendment and interpretation

## Standing of this document

The principles and engineering articles are static normative guidance. Publication of a revision, possession of a copy, a passing check, or a repository merge does **not** by itself establish adoption by an organization or project, authorize activity, or override law, security controls, existing contractual duties or separately reserved decisions.

An adopting authority must identify an exact revision, the scope and effective point of adoption, applicable controlling rules, reserved powers, and any approved exceptions or transition provisions. Older revision identities and historical evidence remain valid references to what applied at their recorded times.

## Authority and roles

**Proposer:** any authorized contributor may suggest an amendment. Proposal authority is not adoption authority.

**Inspector:** examines consistency, evidence, regressions, impacts and plausible alternatives. Inspection is not certification.

**Independent qualifier:** where required, assesses the amendment against independently specified obligations and records a scoped result. Independence cannot be inferred from a second automated process alone.

**Adopting authority:** a competent identified individual or body determines whether, when and to what scope an amendment is adopted. Human approval alone is insufficient without valid authority and adequate context.

**Integrator / custodian:** publishes the accepted canonical text and records lineage, without expanding the adopting authority's decision.

One individual may perform multiple roles where the applicable risk and governing rules allow it. Required independence may not be waived through role renaming or delegation.

## Amendment procedure

1. **Propose.** Identify exact predecessor revision, affected principles/articles, reason, evidence, intended semantic changes and alternatives.
2. **Inspect.** Assess constitutional coherence, authority, external boundary commitments, evidence continuity, feasibility and backward-compatibility risks.
3. **Review proportionately.** Apply the highest relevant risk tier. Material changes to authority, certification, constitutional floors or historical semantics require focused independent examination.
4. **Decide.** The competent adopting authority accepts, rejects or defers a specified text and scope. A technical merge cannot stand in for this decision.
5. **Canonicalize.** Publish a distinct revision with ancestry, change record, effective conditions and accountable acceptance. Preserve predecessor bytes and rejected material as required by law and legitimate review value.
6. **Adopt locally.** Each adopting context makes or records its own revision selection and transition; a newly published text does not silently amend existing frozen contracts.
7. **Correct or supersede.** Material defects, new evidence, demonstrated contradictions or competent instruction may justify controlled reopening. Preserve original decision lineage and explain the successor.

## Semantic versioning and transitions

Record a stable revision identifier and exact content identity. Classify each amendment as editorial, clarifying or normative. A normative amendment must specify whether it changes duties or permitted behavior, what dependencies are affected and whether prior arrangements need explicit renegotiation. Do not relabel a substantive change as editorial to avoid review.

Where an amendment modifies a relied-upon external commitment, identify the compatibility path or lawful exception. If compatibility cannot be established, do not claim transparent migration.

## Evidence, retention and correction

Retain the original text and the decision record for accepted and superseded amendments. Keep materially relevant failed inspections, objections and disagreements with distinct identities. Apply lawful access restrictions, data minimization and retention requirements. Historical evidence is not retroactively recertified by a newer constitution.

## Non-authorizing character

Nothing here constitutes spending approval, personnel delegation, credential access, production release, external effect permission, legal certification or transfer of independent qualification authority.
